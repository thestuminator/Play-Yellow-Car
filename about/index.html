About Play Yellow Car

The family road-trip game that gets kids looking out the window.

A ridiculously simple family road-trip game is turning screen time into real-world play — one yellow car at a time.

Stuart Carr & Dr Sabine Mercer — co-creators of Play Yellow Car, Cairns, Australia.
How it started

Born on the road. Built on a rainy Sunday.

Play Yellow Car began quite by accident.

Cairns couple Stuart Carr and Dr Sabine Mercer started playing a ridiculously simple game together on their drives: spot a yellow car first, call “Yellow Car!”, and score a point.

There was no business plan, no app and certainly no intention to create a product. It was simply something they loved playing together.

Then they introduced the game to Stuart’s daughter Emmagen and her family.

Her three-and-a-half-year-old son, Thomas, embraced it immediately. Instead of disappearing into a screen during car trips, he began scanning the road, watching the traffic and competing with the adults to find the next yellow car.

“Thomas loves the yellow BMW. It has changed our family drives forever. He’s now competitively looking for yellow cars instead of being glued to his device.”

That was when Stuart and Sabine realised their little game might be doing something unexpectedly useful.

Then came a rainy Sunday morning.

With the rain keeping them indoors, Stuart and Sabine sat down together and turned their game into a simple web app.

The idea behind it was almost the opposite of conventional app design: the screen shouldn’t become the entertainment. It should simply keep the score.

The entertainment is outside the window.

A game they created for themselves became something much bigger when Thomas started playing.

An app that wants you to stop looking at the app

Play Yellow Car deliberately does very little. At the beginning of a journey, family members add the players and start the game. During the trip, a passenger records points whenever someone spots a yellow car.

The phone keeps the score, records the journey and eventually declares a winner. But the game itself happens somewhere else: outside the window.

Children start scanning traffic, watching intersections, examining car parks and competing with Mum, Dad, brothers and sisters to make the next discovery. Suddenly the passing world becomes the entertainment.

The people behind Play Yellow Car

Dr Sabine Mercer holds a PhD in literature. She brings a love of language, ideas and human connection to a game whose magic begins with two simple words shouted out loud.

Stuart brings an enthusiasm for cars, technology and turning ideas into things people can actually use.

Together, they created Play Yellow Car around one very simple principle:

What if the best children’s app is one they barely need to look at?

Technology working backwards

For years, technology companies have worked extraordinarily hard to increase engagement — more scrolling, more watching and more time spent inside applications.

Play Yellow Car asks a different question. The phone becomes the scoreboard rather than the playground. The real world becomes the game.

And Thomas gets some of the credit

The first family edition of Play Yellow Car was created for Thomas, Mum and Dad. Thomas’s enthusiasm for the game — and his family’s observation that it had genuinely changed the atmosphere of their drives — suggested that something created for one little boy might have a much bigger idea hiding inside it.

Play Yellow Car doesn’t promise to solve children’s relationship with technology. It does something considerably simpler. It gives them a reason to look up. To observe. To compete. To laugh with their family.

And perhaps to discover that, every once in a while, what’s happening outside the screen can be more interesting than what’s happening on it.

Play Yellow Car was born on the road, built on a rainy Sunday morning, and found its bigger purpose through a three-and-a-half-year-old boy who started looking out the window.
