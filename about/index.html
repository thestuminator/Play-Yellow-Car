About Yellow Car

The family road-trip game that gets kids looking out the window.

A ridiculously simple family road-trip game is turning screen time into real-world play — one yellow car at a time.

At a time when parents are constantly looking for ways to prise children away from phones, tablets and endless streams of digital entertainment, a deceptively simple game is taking a completely different approach.

Look out the window. Spot a yellow car. Shout “YELLOW CAR!” Score a point.

Most apps compete for a child’s attention by trying to keep their eyes on the screen. Yellow Car uses the screen to get them looking away from it.

Created by
Stuart Carr & Dr Sabine Mercer

A Cairns couple who created their own version of Yellow Car quite by accident, then discovered how much more it could become.

How it started

The idea began quite by accident with Cairns couple Stuart Carr and Dr Sabine Mercer, who created their own version of the simple driving game they called Yellow Car. What started as spontaneous fun soon became a favourite part of their journeys together — spotting yellow cars, calling “Yellow Car!” and competing for points.

They loved the game so much that they introduced it to Stuart’s daughter Emmagen and her family, where it took on a life of its own.

Her three-and-a-half-year-old son, Thomas, quickly became captivated — not on another device, but on spotting yellow cars.

“Thomas loves the yellow BMW. It has changed our family drives forever. He’s now competitively looking for yellow cars instead of being glued to his device.”

An app that wants you to stop looking at the app

Yellow Car deliberately does very little. At the beginning of a journey, family members add the players and start the game. During the trip, a passenger records points whenever someone spots a yellow car.

The phone keeps the score, records the journey and eventually declares a winner. But the game itself happens somewhere else: outside the window.

Children start scanning traffic, watching intersections, examining car parks and competing with Mum, Dad, brothers and sisters to make the next discovery. Suddenly the passing world becomes the entertainment.

Technology working backwards

For years, technology companies have worked extraordinarily hard to increase engagement — more scrolling, more watching and more time spent inside applications.

What if the best children’s app is one they barely need to look at?

The phone becomes the scoreboard rather than the playground. The real world becomes the game.

And Thomas gets some of the credit

The first family edition of Yellow Car was created for Thomas, Mum and Dad. Thomas’s enthusiasm for the game — and his family’s observation that it had genuinely changed the atmosphere of their drives — suggested that something created for one little boy might have a much bigger idea hiding inside it.

Yellow Car doesn’t promise to solve children’s relationship with technology. It does something considerably simpler. It gives them a reason to look up. To observe. To compete. To laugh with their family.

And perhaps to discover that, every once in a while, what’s happening outside the screen can be more interesting than what’s happening on it.
